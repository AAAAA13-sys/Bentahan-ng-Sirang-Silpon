CHAPTER 1 BUSINESS PROFILE 1.1 Name of the Company The business operates under the official name “Silicon Trading: The Smartphone Donor Board Pipeline” . The primary moniker, Silicon Trading, highlights the platform’s core focus on commercializing the intrinsic value of silicon microchips, integrated circuits, and motherboards harvested from electronic scrap. By positioning the brand around "silicon," the name emphasizes the commodity trading of high-demand micro-parts through a centralized digital e-commerce platform. The subtitle, The Smartphone Donor Board Pipeline, reflects the operational mechanics of the e-commerce marketplace. It represents a continuous digital supply channel ("pipeline") bridging local scrap phone collectors with large-scale commercial refurbishers and retail buyers. Through this platform, discarded mobile devices serve as "donor boards," supplying functional components to satisfy the demand for sustainable circular tech manufacturing and e-waste reduction. 1.2 Nature of Business Silicon Trading operates exclusively as a two-sided digital e-commerce marketplace platform. The business acts strictly as an online intermediary connecting buyers and sellers within the secondary electronics scrap and donor board industry. The platform is operated

entirely online and is accessible to users anywhere in the Philippines. ● Digital Platform and Intermediary Services: The platform provides the technology infrastructure and operational support that enable secure transactions between market participants. Its core services include standardized component listings, part-compatibility matching tools, seller verification, escrow-protected payment handling, and courier tracking support. Sellers arrange their own shipping through the courier of their choice, and the platform records and displays the tracking details. ● Business-to-Business (B2B) and Business-to-Consumer (B2C) Marketplace Facilitation: The platform facilitates electronic scrap and component trading across two primary digital channels: ○ Business-to-Business (B2B) Channel: Connects local scrap collectors and buyers directly with large-scale commercial refurbishers and traders who require bulk donor boards for component harvesting and device refurbishment. ○ Business-to-Consumer (B2C) Channel: Provides a direct-to-consumer marketplace where individual technicians, repair shops, and DIY hobbyists can purchase single, low-cost motherboards and micro-parts for personal repairs. ○ Manufacturing Support: While the company itself does not engage in physical scrap processing or manufacturing, the platform serves the circular tech manufacturing sector by establishing an organized digital supply pipeline for reclaimed silicon microchips, integrated circuits, and reusable motherboards. CHAPTER II MARKETING ASPECT This chapter discusses the nature of the unsatisfied demand which this study seeks to meet as well as its growth and the manner in which it is set to be met. The supply-demand situation is examined, the target markets are analysed, and the marketing program is formulated. Industry Background Silicon Trading operates in the digital marketplace and secondary smartphone components sector, supporting repair and refurbishment by trading donor motherboards and reusable integrated circuits. The proposed platform will be registered in Pasig City and operated online, so it is accessible to users anywhere in the Philippines. Metro Manila is the priority target market for promotion and demand analysis, with outreach focused on the repair clusters in Greenhills and Quiapo. It will connect scrap collectors, repair technicians, and commercial refurbishers through standardized listings, compatibility matching, seller verification, and protected transactions, without holding any physical inventory. Widespread mobile device use gives the business a relevant foundation. Preliminary results from the Philippine Statistics Authority's 2024 National Information and Communications Technology Household Survey showed that 98.8% of internet users aged ten and over went online through cellphones. This shows how central mobile devices are in the Philippines, but it does not directly prove demand for donor boards or repair components. International trends also support looking into the repair and reuse market. GSMA's Mobile Net Zero 2025 report notes that used and refurbished phone sales rose 15% from 2021 to 2023,

=== PAGE 15 ===
while new smartphone sales fell 15%. ITU and UNITAR also reported that global electronic waste reached 62 million tonnes in 2022, of which only 22.3% was documented as formally collected and recycled. Together, these trends give a broader commercial and environmental case for extending device life and reusing components. Against this background, Silicon Trading proposes a specialized online platform that makes donor components easier to find and trade. Its market research will test whether buyers and sellers value its compatibility tools, verification services, and 72-hour inspection escrow. The cited indicators are background only and do not fully describe Metro Manila's donor-board market, so local demand, supply, and willingness to pay must be validated separately. Market Research This study will use descriptive market research, gathering data through questionnaires, interviews, and secondary sources, to assess Silicon Trading's proposed marketplace in Metro Manila. Initial fieldwork will focus on Greenhills and Quiapo. Respondents will include commercial refurbishers, repair shops, independent technicians, scrap collectors, and consolidators. The study will look at their component needs, supply availability, current sourcing difficulties, and willingness to use the platform. Buyer and supplier lists will be built from business directories and field listing. A separate simple random sample will be drawn from each list using randomly selected ID numbers, so every listed unit has an equal chance of being chosen (Statistics Canada, n.d.). Because fieldwork is limited to Greenhills and Quiapo, findings will apply only to the populations on these lists, not to all of Metro Manila. Demand Analysis The market demand for smartphone repair components—specifically donor motherboards, integrated circuits (ICs), basebands, and power management ICs (PMICs)—is driven by the rapid growth of the secondary smartphone market and the micro-soldering repair industry in Metro Manila. Traditional smartphone repair services rely on sourcing entire replacement assemblies from overseas or informal local channels. However, board-level repairs requiring specific micro-components and donor boards often face severe supply bottlenecks due to fragmented seller networks and unverified part quality. Silicon Trading satisfies this unsatisfied market demand by offering an asset-light, two-sided digital e-commerce marketplace platform that bridges local scrap phone collectors and e-waste consolidators with independent repair technicians, micro-soldering specialists, and commercial refurbishers. The platform provides component compatibility matching tools, single-listing verification passes (₱49/listing or ₱399/monthly bundle), B2B refurbisher subscription tiers (Starter, Pro at ₱2,499/mo, Enterprise at ₱6,999/mo), and a 72-hour escrow settlement mechanism with an 8% commission fee.

Demand for critical donor logic boards is relatively inelastic. CHAPTER V TECHNICAL ASPECT This chapter discusses the nature of the product line, the technology necessary for its production availability. It also discusses the proper mix of production resources, the optimum production volume, and the proper disposal of waste of the company Production/Service Process Silicon Trading is an asset-light, two-sided online marketplace. It does not manufacture, process, store, or ship any product. Its "production" is therefore a service process: the steps that take a reclaimed donor board or component from a verified seller to a buyer in a safe, standardized, and traceable way. The platform provides the technology that supports each step, namely seller verification, standardized listings, compatibility matching, escrow-protected payment, tracking records, and dispute handling. Physical handling and shipping remain with the sellers, who book their own couriers. The process involves four participants: the Seller, the Silicon Trading Platform, the Buyer, and the Courier Partner. Figure 2 shows the full process.

Stage 1: Seller Onboarding and Verification The process begins when a seller, such as a scrap collector, repair technician, or small repair shop, registers an account and submits the required identification and business details. The platform reviews the submission. If verification fails, the seller corrects the information and resubmits it. Only verified sellers may create listings. This protects buyers from fraudulent sellers and helps the company keep its transaction dispute rate below 5%. Stage 2: Listing Creation and Optional Verified Pass A verified seller creates a standardized listing. It includes photos, specifications, condition, price, and compatibility information, such as the phone model and board revision. The seller may then add the Verified Pass ( ₱ 49 per listing, or ₱ 399 per month for eight listings). If purchased, the listing receives three additional services: ● A Verified IC Diagnostic Badge, confirming that the Power Management IC, Audio IC, and Baseband show no physical cracking, stripped pads, or heat stress. ● Automated compatibility tagging against compatible repair models. ● Priority search indexing, which places the listing at the top of refurbisher searches. Listings without the pass skip this step and go straight to publishing. All approved listings are then published on the marketplace. Stage 3: Buyer Search and Ordering Buyers include technicians, repair shops, and commercial refurbishers. A buyer logs in, searches by phone model or component type, and checks compatibility. Before ordering, the buyer reviews the price, component condition, seller verification status, and transaction details. The buyer then places the order.

=== PAGE 83 ===
Stage 4: Escrow Payment Once the order is placed, the platform holds the buyer's payment in escrow for the 72-hour inspection period and notifies the seller to ship the item. The funds are not released to the seller at this point. This protects the buyer's capital until the component has been checked. Stage 5: Fulfilment and Delivery The seller arranges shipping on their own. They book a courier of their choice (such as LBC, J&T, or Lalamove), pack the board, and hand it over for delivery. The seller then enters the tracking number on the platform, so the buyer can follow the delivery under Orders. Silicon Trading does not book couriers, generate waybills, or handle parcels. It only records and displays the tracking information that the seller provides. The courier picks up the parcel and delivers it to the buyer. Stage 6: Inspection and Settlement When the buyer receives the board, the 72-hour inspection window begins. The buyer checks the board against the listing, including impedance and pad integrity under a microscope. The result goes one of two ways. 1. Specifications match. The platform releases the payment to the seller, minus the applicable platform fee. The fee is 8% for non-subscription transactions. For subscribed buyers it is 10% for Starter Refurbisher, 5% for Pro Workshop, and 0% for Enterprise Refurbisher. The transaction is then complete. 2. Specifications do not match. The buyer files a dispute through the Support section with photos or other evidence. The platform reviews the dispute. If it is upheld, the platform instructs the buyer to return the board through a courier. After the seller confirms receipt of the returned board, the platform refunds the buyer from escrow and closes the dispute.
